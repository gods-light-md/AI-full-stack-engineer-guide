# 15 Mistakes Learners Make (and the exact fix)

Ordered roughly by *when* they tend to hit you — early, mid, and late in the journey.

### 1. Tutorial hopping instead of finishing one path
Jumping between 3 different "best AI roadmap" videos/courses because the current one feels slow.
**Fix:** Pick one primary resource per module and finish it before evaluating alternatives. Cross-referencing is fine; abandoning is not.

### 2. Collecting courses instead of building projects
A learner with 12 completed courses and zero shipped projects is *less* employable than one with 2 courses and 3 shipped projects.
**Fix:** For every course/module, the deliverable is a project, not a certificate. No project = module not actually done.

### 3. Skipping math "because you can just call `.fit()`"
Works until you hit a debugging wall, an interview question, or a paper you need to read.
**Fix:** You don't need proofs — you need intuition. Spend the 5 weeks in Phase 1; it pays back with interest every module after.

### 4. Learning frameworks before fundamentals
Jumping straight to PyTorch/LangChain without understanding what a gradient or an embedding actually is.
**Fix:** Build one thing from scratch (a tiny neural net, a basic vector search) before using the library that automates it. It's the difference between using a tool and understanding it.

### 5. Perfectionism on Project 1
Spending 6 weeks polishing a beginner heart-disease-prediction notebook until it's "portfolio perfect."
**Fix:** Ship at 80%. Early projects exist to teach the pipeline, not to impress recruiters — your capstone is what impresses recruiters.

### 6. Ignoring version control until "the project is more serious"
Losing work, or having a GitHub profile that only shows your last 2 months of activity.
**Fix:** `git init` on day one of everything, always, no exceptions.

### 7. Not specializing — trying to be equally deep in CV, NLP, GenAI, MLOps, and mobile
Current hiring data shows roles have fragmented into specific tracks (Applied ML, Platform/MLOps, LLM Engineer, etc.); generalist-with-no-depth candidates lose to hybrid specialists.
**Fix:** Go broad at a working-knowledge level across the stack, but pick ONE track (most likely GenAI/LLMs in 2026) to go genuinely deep in.

### 8. Training models only in notebooks, never deploying anything
A model in a Jupyter notebook is a science project. A model behind an API is a product.
**Fix:** Every project after Phase 2 must end with something reachable by a URL, not just a `.ipynb` file.

### 9. Copy-pasting from AI tools without understanding the output
Using Claude/ChatGPT/Copilot to generate code you can't explain in an interview.
**Fix:** Use AI tools to accelerate, never to substitute understanding. After generating code, rewrite one function yourself from memory as a check.

### 10. Comparing your Week 8 to someone else's Year 2
Social media (ironically, given `04`) makes everyone look 3 steps ahead.
**Fix:** Track your own week-over-week delta only. Nobody's public highlight reel shows their debugging sessions.

### 11. Underestimating data cleaning and overestimating model choice
Spending days tuning hyperparameters on a model fed bad data, chasing 1% accuracy gains that don't matter.
**Fix:** 60–70% of real ML work is data quality. Assume this from Project 1 onward.

### 12. Neglecting evaluation rigor (data leakage, wrong metrics, no baseline)
Reporting "95% accuracy" on an imbalanced dataset where predicting the majority class alone gets 94%.
**Fix:** Always establish a naive baseline first, always check for leakage, always pick a metric that matches the actual problem (precision/recall, not just accuracy).

### 13. Burning out from unsustainable pace in month 1
Doing 4 hours a day for 3 weeks, then dropping to zero for 2 months.
**Fix:** Pick a pace you could sustain for a year, not a month. 10 consistent hours beats 25 sprint-then-crash hours.

### 14. Waiting to "feel ready" before applying for jobs or sharing work
Perpetually feeling "not quite there yet" even after a genuinely solid capstone.
**Fix:** Start applying and posting once your capstone is deployed and documented — readiness is a moving target you'll never fully hit; evidence (shipped work) is not.

### 15. Treating deployment/DevOps/MLOps as optional "extra" learning
Assuming the model itself is the hard/impressive part and infra is a footnote.
**Fix:** In 2026's market, MLOps and deployment skill is one of the fastest-growing, hardest-to-fill demand areas — treat modules 10, 13, and 14 in your roadmap as core, not bonus.

---

### Bonus pattern to watch for (not counted in the 15, but common)
**Optimizing your learning setup instead of learning.** Rebuilding your Notion dashboard, trying a 4th note-taking app, redesigning your GitHub README template — repeatedly — is productive-feeling procrastination. If you've changed tools more than twice in a month, that's the signal, not a coincidence.
